1. Product Definition and Vision
The Fisher Community Market Access & Weather Safety Portal is a global, location-aware web platform that combines fishing-market transparency, catch discovery, weather information, marine conditions, safety advisories, and direct buyer-fisher connections in one place.
Core idea
Anyone can LOOK at public information. Only an Admin-approved Fisher can LIST catch, and only an Admin-approved Buyer can submit purchase requests or initiate protected marketplace interactions.

1.1 Product loop
Select / detect location        ↓View weather + marine conditions + safety advisories        ↓View fish prices and market trends        ↓Fisher decides when/where to fish        ↓Fisher catches fish and publishes a catch listing        ↓Buyer searches/filter listings        ↓Buyer sends purchase request        ↓Fisher accepts/rejects        ↓Deal proceeds outside the platform payment system        ↓Listing is marked sold/closed

2. Problem Statement
Fishers may have limited visibility into current market prices and therefore have weaker negotiating power.
Dependency on intermediaries can reduce fisher margins and limit direct buyer relationships.
Weather and sea conditions can change rapidly, creating safety risks when decisions are made without timely information.
Buyers may struggle to discover available catch directly from local or regional fishers.
Fishing information is often fragmented across different sources instead of being presented in a single location-aware interface.
2.1 Product response
Problem
Portal response
Opaque prices
Location-aware fish price dashboard with timestamp, source and historical trend data.
Middleman dependency
Public catch marketplace plus direct buyer-fisher connection.
Sea/weather uncertainty
Weather + marine conditions + visible safety warnings and source timestamps.
Buyer discovery
Search/filter marketplace with fish type, quantity, location and freshness.
Fragmented information
Single dashboard organized around a chosen location.


3. Goals and Objectives
3.1 Primary objectives
Provide transparent, location-aware fish market price information.
Enable fishers to publish available catch for public discovery.
Enable approved buyers to find catch and send purchase requests.
Present current weather, forecasts and marine conditions for a chosen location.
Present safety alerts and fishing advisories without claiming that the system guarantees safety.
Centralize fishing-community information in a responsive web portal.
3.2 Secondary objectives
Support price trends by fish species and market.
Support global location search using latitude/longitude.
Allow admins to curate fish species, prices and advisories.
Provide notifications for approvals, buyer requests, weather alerts and marketplace events.
Record operational KPIs and administrative audit activity.

4. Scope and Phase Boundaries
4.1 In scope - Phase 1
Guest browsing of location-based weather, marine conditions, fish prices and public catch listings.
Fisher registration, approval, login, profile management and catch listing.
Buyer registration, approval, login, profile management and marketplace search.

Fish species master catalogue.

Admin management panel.

4.2 Out of scope - Phase 1
Online payment processing or escrow.
GPS-based live boat tracking.
IoT ocean-sensor integration.
Native Android/iOS application.
Automated maritime navigation or route guidance.
Guaranteeing safe fishing conditions or replacing official emergency services.
Complex internal chat platform; Phase 1 uses purchase requests/contact workflow.
4.3 Phase-1 page requirement
The implementation should contain at least 6-8 interconnected user-facing/admin pages. The specification below expands that into a practical page set while keeping the required pages identifiable.

6. End-to-End Logical Workflow
6.1 Global user journey
Landing Page   ↓Choose / Search Location   ↓Location Overview   ├── Weather   ├── Marine Conditions   ├── Safety Alerts   ├── Fish Prices   └── Catch Marketplace          │          ├── Guest → continue browsing          │          └── Registered approved user → protected actions
6.2 Fisher workflow
Register as Fisher.
Account enters pending state; Fisher cannot list catch yet.
Admin reviews the registration and approves or rejects it.
Approved Fisher completes or updates profile and location.
Fisher checks weather, marine conditions, alerts and market prices.
Fisher creates a catch listing with species, quantity, catch date, location, freshness and asking price.
Listing becomes publicly visible when valid and active.
Approved buyers can view the listing and submit a purchase request.
Fisher reviews incoming request and accepts, rejects or otherwise closes it.
On completion, Fisher or authorized workflow marks the listing sold/closed.
6.3 Buyer workflow
Register as Buyer.
Account enters pending state.
Admin verifies and approves/rejects the account.
Approved Buyer searches the marketplace by fish, location, quantity, price and freshness.
Buyer opens a listing and views relevant fisher information.
Buyer submits a purchase request containing quantity, offered price and message.
Fisher receives notification and reviews the request.
Fisher accepts/rejects; both parties receive status notifications.
The actual sale and payment occur outside the Phase-1 platform.
6.4 Admin workflow
Login to protected admin area.
Review pending Fisher and Buyer registrations.
Approve, reject, suspend or deactivate accounts.
Manage fish species catalogue.
Add/update/verify market price records.
Monitor and moderate public catch listings.
Create/manage safety alerts and advisories.
View operational metrics and audit logs.

8. Detailed Functional Requirements
Authentication & Accounts
FR-AUTH-01: User can register as exactly one Phase-1 role: Fisher or Buyer.
FR-AUTH-02: Admin accounts are provisioned securely and are not created through public registration.
FR-AUTH-03: Newly registered Fisher/Buyer accounts have pending approval status.
FR-AUTH-04: Pending users cannot perform protected marketplace actions.
FR-AUTH-05: Admin can approve or reject pending registrations and must record the decision time and optional reason.
FR-AUTH-06: Approved users can authenticate using secure password-based authentication.
FR-AUTH-07: Suspended/deactivated accounts cannot use protected APIs.
Fisher
FR-FISH-01: Fisher can maintain profile, fishing location and boat details.
FR-FISH-02: Fisher can view prices, weather, marine data and alerts for selected locations.
FR-FISH-03: Fisher can create, edit, cancel and close own catch listings.
FR-FISH-04: Fisher cannot create a listing while account is not approved.
FR-FISH-05: Fisher can review incoming purchase requests on own listings.
FR-FISH-06: Fisher receives notifications for requests and important platform alerts.
Buyer
FR-BUY-01: Buyer can browse public listings without an account.
FR-BUY-02: Approved Buyer can submit purchase requests.
FR-BUY-03: Purchase request must reference an active listing and valid quantity.
FR-BUY-04: Buyer can view own requests and statuses.
FR-BUY-05: Buyer can receive status notifications.
Market Prices
FR-PRICE-01: Public users can search prices by location, market and species.
FR-PRICE-02: Price records must include update time and source metadata.
FR-PRICE-03: Admin can create/update/verify price records.
FR-PRICE-04: Historical records can feed trend charts.
FR-PRICE-05: Currency must be explicit; the UI must not silently assume the local currency.
Weather & Marine
FR-WX-01: User can select/search a location and retrieve current weather.
FR-WX-02: User can view forecast information.
FR-WX-03: User can view wind, temperature, precipitation, visibility and related fields available from the configured provider.
FR-WX-04: User can view wave height, wave direction, wave period, swell and sea-surface temperature where marine data is available.
FR-WX-05: System displays source and data timestamp/freshness information.
FR-WX-06: Safety advisories are visibly distinct from ordinary forecast data.
FR-WX-07: The interface does not state that a forecast guarantees safe conditions.
Administration
FR-ADM-01: Admin can approve/reject users.
FR-ADM-02: Admin can suspend/deactivate users.
FR-ADM-03: Admin can manage species.
FR-ADM-04: Admin can manage market price data.
FR-ADM-05: Admin can manage safety alerts.
FR-ADM-06: Admin can moderate catch listings.
FR-ADM-07: Admin can view reports and audit logs.

9. Location and Globalization Model
The portal is global by design. No module should assume India, a particular currency, or a fixed set of countries. A location should resolve to coordinates and a standard place representation.
9.1 Location selection
User enters a city/place/postal code or selects a map/location result.
Geocoding resolves the search to latitude, longitude, country, region and timezone.
The selected location ID/coordinates become the context for weather, marine data, market lookup and nearby listings.
Guests can repeat the process for any supported location.
9.2 Coordinates
MongoDB GeoJSON Point{  type: "Point",  coordinates: [longitude, latitude]}Use a 2dsphere index for spherical proximity queries.
MongoDB documents that GeoJSON coordinates use longitude first and latitude second, and that 2dsphere indexes support earth-like spherical proximity queries. [MongoDB documentation]
9.3 Globalization fields
Country and ISO country code.
State/province/region where applicable.
Timezone.
Currency code for market/listing prices.
Localized fish names where available.
Metric/imperial display preference if added later.

10. Weather and Marine Data Architecture
10.1 Provider model
Use a provider abstraction in the backend so the UI never depends directly on a third-party API response shape. The backend normalizes external fields into the application schema.
React UI   ↓Express API   ↓Weather/Machine Data Service   ├── Weather Provider (e.g., Tomorrow.io)   └── Marine/Forecast Provider (e.g., Open-Meteo Marine)   ↓Normalized response + cache   ↓React UI
10.2 Weather data
Temperature / apparent temperature.
Humidity and pressure where available.
Wind speed and direction.
Precipitation amount/probability.
Visibility.
Cloud cover / UV where available.
Current conditions and hourly/daily forecast.
Provider source and fetched/observed timestamps.
10.3 Marine data
Wave height.
Wave direction.
Wave period.
Wind-wave metrics where available.
Swell height/direction/period.
Sea-surface temperature.
Ocean current velocity/direction where available.
Sea-state classification derived by application rules, with the underlying numeric data preserved.
Open-Meteo currently documents a Marine API that exposes wave height, wave direction, wave period, swell variables, sea-surface temperature and ocean-current variables. Its documented marine datasets are model-based and have different update frequencies; the provider explicitly warns that some coastal data are not suitable for navigation. [Open-Meteo Marine API]
10.4 Current vs forecast semantics
Important
“Current” in an API does not necessarily mean a live sensor reading at the exact requested coordinate. The UI must show the provider, observation/model timestamp and last refresh time. Weather forecasts are model outputs; marine coverage and quality can vary by region.

Tomorrow.io documents a Realtime Weather endpoint that returns current weather information for a requested location, with a minute-level temporal resolution in the API description. Open-Meteo documents current/forecast weather and marine model outputs whose source models are updated on schedules rather than continuously at every coordinate. [Tomorrow.io Realtime Weather; Open-Meteo model and marine docs]
10.5 Safety disclaimer requirement
The portal may classify conditions as informational labels such as Low / Moderate / High risk, but safety-critical warnings should carry their external source and timestamp. The application must not present a generated classification as an official maritime safety warning unless the underlying source explicitly provides that warning.

12. Marketplace and Buyer-Fisher Connection
12.1 Catch listing lifecycle
DRAFT → ACTIVE → RESERVED / PARTIALLY_SOLD → SOLD                       │                       ├→ EXPIRED                       └→ CANCELLED / REMOVED
12.2 Listing rules
Only approved Fisher accounts can create listings.
Listing must reference an active fish species and valid catch location.
Quantity must be greater than zero.
Catch date cannot be impossible or outside defined business rules.
Asking price must be non-negative and currency required.
Only the owning Fisher or authorized Admin can edit/remove a listing.
Inactive, sold, expired or removed listings cannot receive new purchase requests.
Public viewers can see only data intended to be public; private contact details should not be exposed unnecessarily.
12.3 Purchase request lifecycle
PENDING → ACCEPTED → COMPLETED       └→ REJECTED       └→ CANCELLED
12.4 Phase-1 transaction boundary
A purchase request is an expression of buyer intent and a connection mechanism. It is not a payment transaction. Payment, invoicing, escrow, shipping and legal trade settlement are outside Phase 1.

20. Authentication and Security
20.1 Authentication
Hash passwords using Argon2id or bcrypt with a strong work factor.
Use HTTPS in production.
Use short-lived access tokens or secure server-side sessions; refresh-token strategy should be implemented securely if JWT is chosen.
Never store access tokens in insecure long-lived browser storage when a safer HTTP-only cookie architecture is practical.
Rate-limit login and registration endpoints.
Return generic authentication errors to avoid account enumeration.
20.2 Authorization
isAuthenticated      ↓accountStatus === approved?      ↓role allowed for endpoint?      ↓resource ownership check?      ↓service operation
20.3 Data protection
Never return passwordHash.
Protect private contact data from public listing APIs.
Do not log passwords, tokens or API keys in audit logs.
Sanitize and validate uploaded image/file metadata.
Validate URLs before storing public image/source links.
Use environment variables for external API credentials.
Use security headers (e.g., Helmet in Express) and strict CORS configuration.
Add request size limits and input sanitization against injection/XSS/NoSQL-injection risks.
20.4 External API keys
Third-party API credentials belong in the backend environment, never in React source code. The browser should call your API; your API calls the provider.

22. Caching, Freshness and External-API Resilience
22.1 Why caching is required
Do not call a third-party weather API directly on every component render or every visitor interaction. That would waste quota, increase latency and make the site fragile. Cache by location/time window and let the backend serve a consistent normalized response.
22.2 Suggested cache strategy
Data
Suggested approach
Freshness principle
Current weather
Short TTL cache per location/forecast cell
Refresh when stale; show fetchedAt/observedAt.
Hourly forecast
Longer TTL than current weather
Refresh when provider model changes or cache expires.
Marine data
Cache per location + model timestamp
Show source/model timestamp; avoid implying live sensor data.
Safety alerts
Short TTL + explicit validity dates
Prefer fresher warning feed; do not hide active alerts.
Market prices
Persist latest records + history
Show market date and updated timestamp.

22.3 Provider fallback
Primary provider unavailable → serve last known cached data with a visible stale indicator when the data is still acceptable.
No usable data → show a clear unavailable state rather than fabricated values.
Never silently substitute a different source without marking the source.
Use retries with exponential backoff and hard request timeouts.
Centralize provider-specific logic so switching providers does not change React components.
22.4 Verified provider facts for current implementation
Open-Meteo currently states that its free/open-access API is for non-commercial use and is limited to 10,000 calls/day, 5,000/hour and 600/minute; its documentation also states that weather model data are typically updated every few hours. Open-Meteo provides global marine model products but their update frequencies vary by model. [Open-Meteo pricing/model/marine docs]
Tomorrow.io currently documents a Realtime Weather API and a Free API plan with 500 requests/day, 25/hour and 3 requests/second. These limits and plan terms can change, so the production project should verify the provider terms at deployment time. [Tomorrow.io Realtime Weather and Free Plan docs]

AC-14: Protected APIs enforce authentication, approval status, role and resource ownership.
AC-15: Admin actions are captured in audit logs.
AC-16: Application is mobile-responsive and deployed to a live HTTPS URL for final evaluation.
